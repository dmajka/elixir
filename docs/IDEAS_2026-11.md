## 2. The phaser: why it costs more, and the cleanup (agreed: Phase 10)

Two separate 4-stage all-pass chains (one a side), 64-bit multiplies, a clamp after every stage, the coefficient
every sample. The flanger and ring mod are one read or one multiply a sample.

| Change | Saves | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| One mono chain; L = dry + wet, R = dry − wet (interleaved notches, the classic stereo phaser trick) | ~half | different stereo image (A/B it) |
| Clamp only the feedback (all-passes keep the level) | ~15–20% | none |
| 32-bit math (states in sample units, coefficient Q14) | ~25% | inaudible |
| Coefficient every 8 samples | small | none at phaser rates |

Together ~11% → ~4–5%. **Done (Phase 10):** ~4.5% estimated (96 device instructions a stereo sample against ~236 + 2
divides); the 32-bit math was dropped (pi32v2 multiplies 32 × 32 → 64 at no extra cost; Q14 overflowed). Details in
the TO_DO file.

## 3. Effects

### Agreed (Phases 11–14)
- **GRAIN** delay mode (Clouds-style texture on the existing 64 KB line), **SHIMMER** reverb model, **FOLLOW**
  (envelope follower) global LFO wave, **SHIFT** (frequency shifter) FX bus type. designs in the TO_DO file (Phases 10–15).

### Parked ideas (cheap to medium)
- **VOWEL** DJ type: 2–3 band-passes on formant tables, FILT morphs a-e-i-o-u. ~5%, ~1 KB. Makes kicks and toms talk.
- **Bus bit/rate crush per send** (the dirt code on the FX send only). ~0.5%.
- **GRAIN FREEZE** if not in Phase 13: stop writing the line, grains keep playing (Clouds' freeze).
- **Track-hit triggers for the global LFOs** (TRIG = D1 … HH): an LFO in ONE mode becomes an AD envelope fired by a
  hit. Tiny. (See §5.)
- **MONO / PING PONG to ~110% feedback** through the tape soft clip (the dub note in the TO_DO file).
- Not for this hardware: convolution, spectral freeze, anything FFT-based (RAM and CPU).

### Clouds notes
Mutable Instruments Clouds (Émilie Gillet, MIT licence, compatible with GPL-3.0) runs granular playback on a 1–4 s
buffer at 32 kHz with up to ~40–60 grains; ports (Daisy, various Eurorack DSP modules) keep 1–2 s and fewer grains.
Elixir's version is written from scratch on the delay's line (0.74 s at 44.1 kHz): 6–8 grains is the target. What
makes Clouds sound like Clouds: random grain position and pan, a smooth window, pitch per grain, a **feedback**
path, then a diffuser/reverb after it (ECHO > REV plays that role here), and FREEZE.

## 4. Scene morph vs the kit morph you already have

**Most of a "scene morph" already exists.** MORPH blends the edit buffer with the morph target kit (LOAD > MORPH,
CC 125) over every sound parameter, and the global LFOs push it. The parameters excluded from the blend stay the
kit's: the outputs, the modulation targets, FX type, reverb model, the voice models, LFO modes, delay pitch, dirt
modes.

What a scene morph would add on top (none planned):
1. **Performance state, not just the kit:** the DJ filter (and its type), DENSITY, the global LFOs' depths, macro
   values, master comp, mutes. These live in the project, so today morph can't reach them. This is the genuinely new
   part: ~100 B RAM a scene, ~1.5 KB flash.
2. **Snapshots without saving a kit:** "SNAP" puts the sound as it is now into the morph target in one press, and
   "SWAP" exchanges A and B. Both are cheap (~200 B flash; `kit_save_ram`'s mixture already exists).
3. **More than two points:** 4 scenes on one knob (A→B→C→D). RAM is a kit copy (476 B) a scene.
4. **The discrete parameters switching at the midpoint** (FX type, reverb model) as an option. Risky: clicks.

Drafted as Phase 15 in the TO_DO file for review. Verdict: only (2) is clearly worth it soon. (1) is worth it if you want morph to move the DJ filter and density
together with the sound; but global LFOs on MORPH + DENSITY (what you're doing) already cover much of that.

## 5. Envelope follower vs "envelope-driven modulation"

Same family, three different things:
- **X-MOD (exists):** a voice's output envelope (`venv`) modulates *another voice's* filter or gain. Voice-to-voice,
  inside the kit.
- **FOLLOW wave (agreed, Phase 11):** a global LFO whose output *is* a voice's (or the mix's) level, so a hit can
  push any global destination: the kick ducks the reverb, the snare opens the DJ filter, the hats push density or
  morph. It follows the real sound (velocity, decay, choke, ratchets).
- **Hit-triggered one-shot (parked, §3):** an LFO in ONE mode fired by a track's hit: a clean AD shape of your
  choosing, independent of how the sound decays.

"Envelope-driven modulation" in the earlier top-5 meant the general idea; FOLLOW is the concrete build.

## 7. Generative track modes (research; parked)

Existing: Euclid, Turing, cellular, Grids-style, melody. Sequencer modes cost flash (~1–2 KB each) but no audio CPU.

- **ANSWER / complement:** a track that plays where another doesn't (or inverts it, or echoes it a step later with
  a chance). Call-and-response between kick and percussion.
- **Split loop lengths (Kria-style):** separate lengths for trigger, velocity, probability and lock lanes, so they
  drift apart and realign (polymeter inside one track).
- **Markov LEARN:** record a bar of what you play, then improvise in its style (transition table of steps / notes).
- Others to consider: **L-system / rewriting** patterns; **gravity / bouncing ball** (accelerating ratchets);
  **rain** (Poisson hits with density); **mutation on repeat** (each pass changes N steps, with a lock to keep the
  good ones).

## 8. MICRO (microsound) voice refinements (research; parked)

See docs/MICROSOUND.md for the voices as built.

- **Per-hit variation:** small random grain pitch, position and density on each strike (a VARY amount), so no two
  hits are identical.
- **Velocity → density and brightness**, not just level.
- **Time distribution:** an exponential / scatter option beside the even one.
- **Cloud release:** grains keep firing briefly past the gate end (a tail).
- **Grain envelope shape** (sharper for clicks, smoother for clouds).
